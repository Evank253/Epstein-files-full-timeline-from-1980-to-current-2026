## 4. Critical distinctions

1. **SAR aggregate ≠ proven criminal proceeds.** A SAR reports suspicious activity; it is not proof that each included transaction was unlawful or that the aggregate amount represents illicit funds.
2. **Reported transactions ≠ unique payments.** The underlying methodology may include multiple legs, reversals, checks, internal transfers, transactions passing through other banks, or overlapping reporting categories. The public reporting does not provide a transaction-level reconciliation.
3. **21 SARs ≠ 21 independent investigations or findings.** The count describes filings as reported by journalists.
4. **$581.9m vs. $582m** is consistent with rounding. The reported transaction count differs (approximately 3,000 vs. 3,200); retain the discrepancy rather than silently choosing one.
5. **2019 filing date ≠ transaction date.** The reporting says the SARs were filed in 2019 and covered transactions from an earlier period. Exact filing dates, lookback periods, and covered transaction sets require source records.
6. **Alleged funding sources ≠ proven source of every payment.** Any references to named individuals must be tied to exact transactions and the bank's own characterization; no inference of criminal conduct from being a source of funds.
7. **The leak is not acquired evidence.** EP-01 has not received, hashed, authenticated, or independently reviewed the underlying leaked documents. No sensitive or private bank records are reproduced here.
