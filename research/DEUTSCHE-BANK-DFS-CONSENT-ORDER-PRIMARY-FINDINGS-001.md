## 1. Why this source matters

Unlike recent investigative reporting, the DFS consent order is a primary regulatory document setting out the Department's findings following its investigation of Deutsche Bank's relationship with Epstein and related entities. The order resolves the matter by consent and is not a criminal judgment against every person mentioned. It expressly reserves questions about the ultimate purpose of payments and cash for criminal authorities.

The order states that Deutsche Bank maintained the Epstein relationship from August 2013 through December 2018 (¶8, PDF page 4) and that the Department found the Bank failed in various respects to meet AML/customer-monitoring obligations (¶7, PDF page 3).

## 2. Verified transaction and account findings

### DB-REG-001 — Butterfly Trust beneficiaries and wires

**Locator:** ¶¶28–32, PDF pages 8–9.

- Deutsche Bank opened checking and money-market accounts for the Butterfly Trust on January 24, 2014 (¶28).
- The order states the beneficiaries included three individuals whom the Department labels alleged co-conspirators, plus women with Eastern European surnames. The order records Epstein's representation that beneficiaries were employees or friends (¶28).
- The order says the Bank's KYC records described the money-market account purpose as paying trust expenses/disbursements (¶28).
- The order states Epstein used the Butterfly Trust and other accounts to send **more than 120 wires totaling $2.65 million** to Butterfly Trust beneficiaries, including transfers to alleged co-conspirators or women with Eastern European surnames. Stated purposes included hotel expenses, tuition, and rent (¶31).

**Evidence classification:** Official regulator finding describing the Bank's account records and transaction review.

**Limitations:** The order does not publish a complete transaction schedule, every recipient, or the individual wire references. It does not establish that each payment funded criminal conduct. The label “co-conspirator” in the order must be attributed to the Department's description and not expanded into a new finding of guilt against a person.

### DB-REG-002 — Legal and settlement payments

**Locator:** ¶32, PDF page 9.

The order describes:
- settlement payments totaling **more than $7 million** to law firms; and
- dozens of payments to law firms totaling **more than $6 million** for what appeared to be legal expenses of Epstein and alleged co-conspirators.

**Limitations:** These are categories described by the regulator, not a published itemized ledger. Do not add the $7 million and $6 million as if they were necessarily disjoint populations; the order's wording does not establish whether any transaction overlap exists.

### DB-REG-003 — Cash withdrawals by Epstein's personal attorney

**Locator:** ¶¶48–52, PDF pages 15–17.

The order states:
- Epstein's personal attorney, anonymized in the order as ATTORNEY-1, made **97 withdrawals** at Deutsche Bank's Park Avenue branch from 2013 to 2017 from Epstein's personal accounts (¶48).
- The withdrawals occurred roughly two to three times per month and were each **$7,500**, which the order identifies as the Bank's third-party withdrawal limit (¶48).
- The attorney reportedly said the cash was for travel, tipping, and expenses (¶48).
- In May 2014, the attorney inquired how often cash could be withdrawn without triggering an alert. The order records a relationship coordinator's email asking whether it was once a week, twice a week, or once every other week; the Bank said it had no record of a response (¶50).
- In July 2017, after asking whether a withdrawal above $10,000 required reporting and being told it did, the attorney split a withdrawal over two days (¶51).
- Bank AML personnel discussed suspicions of structuring. The attorney denied intent to structure; Bank personnel found the attorney credible and permitted withdrawals to continue (¶51).
- Shortly before the Park Avenue branch closed in 2018, the attorney withdrew **$100,000** for Epstein, with the explanation reportedly being tipping and household expenses (¶51).
- The order says that in roughly four years the attorney withdrew **more than $800,000** on Epstein's behalf. It also says the Bank filed CTRs appropriately when required, but did not seek or receive an explanation beyond travel, tipping, and expenses (¶52).

**Critical interpretation:** The order records facts that raised structuring concerns and the Bank's internal response. It does not state that ATTORNEY-1 was criminally convicted of structuring. A sequence of $7,500 withdrawals is not, by itself, a finding of criminal intent. Preserve the attorney's denial and the regulator's distinction between CTR compliance and inadequate broader monitoring.

### DB-REG-004 — Monitoring failures and payments involving women

**Locator:** ¶¶41–47 and ¶¶55–59, PDF pages 13–19.

- The order records a March 2017 alert concerning payments to a Russian model and a Russian publicity agent that a monitoring-team member considered normal for this client (¶41).
- The order says the monitoring team was instructed to check whether women involved in transactions were at least 18 and flag transactions only if no rational reason could be discerned (¶42).
- A May 2018 compliance inquiry asked about wires to accounts of women with Eastern European surnames at a Russian bank; the accountant reportedly described a transfer as tuition for a friend (¶46).
- The order also describes payments for lawsuit settlements, rent, legal, and immigration expenses made to or on behalf of young adult women (¶47).
- The Department concluded the Bank did little or nothing to inquire into or block numerous payments to named alleged co-conspirators and to or on behalf of young women, and did not inquire how Epstein used an average of more than $200,000 per year in cash (¶56).
- Crucially, ¶57 states that whether or to what extent the payments or cash were used to cover up old crimes, facilitate new ones, or for another purpose must be left to criminal authorities.

## 3. What this establishes — and what it does not

**Established by this regulatory order:**
1. Deutsche Bank maintained Epstein and related-entity accounts from August 2013 to December 2018.
2. The Bank's records, as summarized by DFS, included Butterfly Trust beneficiary payments, legal/settlement payments, and substantial cash withdrawals.
3. DFS found significant failures in customer due diligence, escalation, transaction monitoring, and risk controls.
4. DFS documented specific cash-withdrawal patterns and internal bank concerns.

**Not established by this order:**
1. That every transaction described was unlawful.
2. That every recipient was involved in criminal conduct.
3. That the $2.65 million, $7 million, $6 million, and cash totals are disjoint and can be summed.
4. That the cash withdrawals were proven criminal structuring.
5. The full identity of every anonymized person in the order.
6. The transaction-level composition or overlap of the 2019 SAR aggregates discussed in later reporting.

## 4. Relationship to the $147m / $582m SAR comparison

This 2020 order provides an earlier, regulator-documented transaction baseline but does not publish the 2019 SAR population or a complete account-level transaction schedule. It cannot be used to validate the Senate Finance Committee's $147 million / 1,140-wire statistic or the November 17 consortium's $581.9–$582 million / 3,000–3,200 transaction aggregate.

The order's 120+ wires / $2.65 million beneficiary figure is a distinct, narrowly described category. It must not be added to later SAR totals without transaction-level deduplication and confirmation of each population's scope.

## 6. Immediate follow-up

1. Use the DFS order's paragraphs and anonymized role labels as a structured lead map.
2. Locate public court/congressional records that lawfully reproduce relevant, redacted transaction schedules or deposition exhibits.
3. Where original DOJ access is available through the ordinary public interface, obtain the PDF without bypassing age verification or other controls, preserve it, hash it, and inspect the exact pages.
4. Keep the 97 withdrawals, $100,000 cash withdrawal, $800,000+ total, 120+ wires / $2.65m, $7m settlements, and $6m legal-expense payments as separate categories until transaction identifiers establish overlap.
5. Do not expose victim identities or private account details in derivative outputs.

**Working conclusion:** The DFS order supplies strong primary regulatory evidence of the scale and nature of Deutsche Bank's documented Epstein-related activity and of serious bank-control failures. It also expressly cautions against deciding the ultimate purpose of payments and cash without criminal-authority findings.
