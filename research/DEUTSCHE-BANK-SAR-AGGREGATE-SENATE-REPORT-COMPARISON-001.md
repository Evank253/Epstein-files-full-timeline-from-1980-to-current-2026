# Deutsche Bank SAR Aggregate — Senate Report Comparison and Reconciliation 001

**Investigation:** EP-01  
**Review date:** 2026-11-17  
**Status:** Official Senate report reviewed; new consortium reporting reviewed; leaked bank records not acquired or authenticated by EP-01.

## 1. Question

How should the newly reported $581.9–$582 million / 3,000–3,200 transaction aggregate be compared with the earlier public figure of approximately $250 million?

## 2. Primary public baseline: Senate Finance Committee

Source: U.S. Senate Committee on Finance, *Looking the Other Way: How Wall Street Banks Enabled Jeffrey Epstein's Sex Trafficking*, August 4, 2026.
Official PDF: https://www.finance.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/wyden_wall_street_epstein_report.pdf

Relevant exact report locators:
- PDF text lines 85–95 (report printed pages 6–7): summary of retroactive SAR activity.
- Report lines 94–95: Deutsche Bank retroactively flagged **1,140 wire transfers totaling $147 million** in and out of Epstein's accounts in 2019.
- Report lines 78–97: methodology; Senate staff reviewed SARs in camera at Treasury on February 14, 2024, and later reviewed unsealed bank records and DOJ records.
- Report lines 113–118: report's description of Bank Secrecy Act SAR timing and monitoring requirements.

The Senate report's $147 million figure is a specifically described population of Deutsche Bank wire transfers. It is not the same as the new consortium's reported $581.9–$582 million aggregate.

The widely repeated approximately $250 million figure is described in the new reporting as the previously public benchmark. The Senate report text reviewed here identifies $147 million for Deutsche Bank's 1,140 wire transfers, and also discusses much larger totals across multiple banks and categories. EP-01 has not yet identified a single Senate-report table or transaction schedule that defines the $250 million as a directly comparable Deutsche Bank-only population. Keep the $250 million benchmark attributed to the reporting until its exact source and scope are reconciled.

## 5. Key interpretation

The new figure should not be described as “$582 million of criminal proceeds,” “$582 million paid to victims,” or “$582 million in wires.” The public consortium reporting describes an aggregate of transactions flagged in 21 SARs. The article's own summary refers to a broader set of transactions than the Senate report's 1,140-wire / $147-million statistic.

The $147 million and $581.9–$582 million figures may be nested, overlapping, or drawn from different classification rules. They must not be added together. The $250 million benchmark also must not be subtracted from $582 million to claim an additional $332 million unless its exact population and deduplication methodology are established.

## 6. Reconciliation protocol

For each reported transaction, a lawful public source must establish, where available:
- unique transaction or bank reference;
- booking and value dates;
- amount and currency;
- payment rail/type (wire, check, internal book transfer, cash, correspondent transaction);
- originating and receiving institution;
- origin and destination account identifiers in appropriately redacted form;
- account holder/entity at the relevant date;
- whether it is a principal transaction, another leg of the same movement, reversal, return, or duplicate;
- which SAR(s) include it and which aggregate it contributes to;
- source-document accession and exact page locator.

Only then can EP-01 calculate unique principal flows, gross transaction volume, net flow, and overlap between reported populations.

## 7. Follow-up priorities

1. Map the Senate report's $147 million / 1,140-wire figure to the specific SARs or public exhibits cited in the report.
2. Identify the source of the $250 million comparison quoted in the November 17 consortium story and establish whether it refers to Deutsche Bank only, wire transfers only, or a broader set.
3. Determine whether the leaked internal summary's 21 SARs include checks, internal transfers, correspondent-bank legs, or duplicate transaction legs.
4. Compare only against lawful public DOJ, court, regulatory, and congressional records; do not obtain or circulate the leaked bank corpus or private account data.
5. Preserve the reported $3,000 vs. $3,200 discrepancy as unresolved until the consortium explains its count methodology or a reliable public primary record does so.

## 8. Current conclusion

The new reporting appears to describe a broader Deutsche Bank transaction population than the Senate report's specifically stated 1,140 wire transfers totaling $147 million. This is a plausible explanation for the larger aggregate, but EP-01 cannot yet confirm the relationship. The reported totals are not additive and are not independently verified transaction totals.
