## 1. Critical corpus limitation

The DOJ Epstein Files Library and DOJ Disclosures are the primary federal source repositories:
- https://www.justice.gov/epstein
- https://www.justice.gov/epstein/doj-disclosures

The repository must ultimately contain a reproducible accession manifest and locally preserved copies of eligible public records, subject to copyright, privacy, redaction, and file-size constraints. It is not accurate to say the entire DOJ corpus is already present. DOJ production totals, page counts, and dataset labels must not be conflated with unique documents. Each file must be individually accessioned, assigned a stable evidence ID, captured with retrieval metadata, hashed, checked for duplicates, and linked to extracted claims and exact locators. Do not bulk-publish victim PII, intimate imagery, illegal sexual-abuse material, or restricted records. Where redistribution is not lawful or appropriate, retain a source pointer, metadata, hash if lawfully captured, and a redaction/handling note.

## 4. MCC custody and cellmate investigation

### Institutional baseline
The DOJ Office of Inspector General’s 2023 report (Report 23-085) is the principal official reconstruction of Epstein’s custody at the Metropolitan Correctional Center, New York, including housing, staff, rounds, surveillance and institutional failures: https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/reports/23-085.pdf. Epstein was housed in the Special Housing Unit in July 2019 until his death on 2019-08-10. MCC closed in 2021. OIG interviewed 15 inmates; 10 were housed in the SHU on Aug 9–10, and three on L Tier had a direct line of sight to Epstein’s cell. Some inmate accounts report not seeing anyone enter or the door open; these are witness accounts, not independent proof of every event. OIG reported that none of the 15 inmates interviewed provided credible information contradicting the FBI’s determination of no criminality connected to the death.

### Nicholas Tartaglione — first cellmate

On July 23 Epstein was found injured with orange cloth around his neck. Tartaglione said he called for help. Preserve all versions and attribute each statement. ## 7. Investigative rules

- Keep primary investigator chronology/graph, independent reinvestigation and independent cross-examination as separate, versioned work products.
- Independent reviewers must reconstruct from source artifacts rather than inherit the first investigator’s conclusions.
- Freeze independent reports before cross-review; no reviewer overwrites another’s source report.
- Label claims: established by primary record; court finding; regulator finding; official agency statement; attributed witness statement; allegation; secondary reporting; inference; disputed; unresolved; not established.
- Graph edges require evidence IDs, relationship type, direction, date interval, confidence/status and a statement of what the source actually supports.
- A person’s appearance in a file, schedule, contact list, flight log, company, bank record or graph does not establish wrongdoing.
- Missing, sealed, redacted, unreviewed or inaccessible evidence is not proof of absence or guilt.
- Never invent source hashes, file acquisition, transaction completion, meeting attendance, ownership, proceeds or legal outcomes.
