# Epstein–Bitcoin Ecosystem Investigation 001

**Investigation ID:** BTC-EPSTEIN-001  
**Opened:** 2026-11-17  
**Status:** Initial documentary leads identified; primary-source verification and financial reconciliation remain in progress.

## 1. Investigative question

What documented financial, institutional, and personal connections did Jeffrey Epstein have to Bitcoin's development and the broader cryptocurrency industry, and what—if anything—do those records establish about influence, control, or authorship?

The investigation separates three questions:

- **Origin/authorship:** Is there reliable evidence Epstein created Bitcoin, co-authored its white paper, operated the Satoshi Nakamoto identity, or participated in the original 2008–2009 protocol development?
- **Developer support:** Did Epstein-sourced donations support MIT Media Lab's Digital Currency Initiative (DCI), which became a home and funding source for Bitcoin Core developers after the Bitcoin Foundation's financial collapse?
- **Commercial exposure:** What investments did Epstein or entities attributed to him make in Coinbase, Blockstream, and other cryptocurrency businesses, through which vehicles, at what dates and amounts, and with what ownership or exit outcomes?

These questions must not be collapsed into one another. A documented investment in a later Bitcoin company does not establish involvement in Bitcoin's original creation.

## 2. Initial evidence-backed findings

### 2.1 MIT and Digital Currency Initiative

MIT's January 10, 2020 independent Goodwin Procter report states that Epstein made ten donations to MIT between 2002 and 2017 totaling $850,000. It identifies $525,000 in post-conviction gifts to the Media Lab and $225,000 to Professor Seth Lloyd. The report does not say that the entire $525,000 funded DCI.

A DOJ-released email dated April 25, 2015, indexed as EFTA00680068, shows Joichi Ito forwarding Epstein a Digital Currency Initiative announcement and writing: “Used gift funds to underwrite this which allowed us to move quickly and win this round.” The forwarded message describes MIT Media Lab's DCI, explains that Bitcoin's core developers were previously paid by the Bitcoin Foundation, and says three developers had decided to join the Media Lab. Epstein replied, “gavin is clever.”

This is direct evidence of Ito telling Epstein that gift funds underwrote the DCI launch. It is not, by itself, a complete accounting trail identifying the exact Epstein donation, amount, bank transfer, restricted/unrestricted fund, or proportion of the initiative's budget attributable to Epstein. The exact donor-to-DCI flow must be reconstructed from MIT gift accounting, correspondence, and bank records. The developers' recruitment and the initiative's work do not establish that Epstein designed Bitcoin, selected protocol rules, directed developers, or controlled Bitcoin Core.

### 2.2 Coinbase investment

DOJ-released correspondence and investment records identify a $3,000,000 Coinbase Series C investment in December 2014 through IGO Company LLC, a U.S. Virgin Islands entity identified in the email chain as the intended investing vehicle. A separate investment allocation document identifies $3,000,001.78 for Crypto Currency Partners II LLC, $200,000.12 for Crypto Currency Partners II LP, and $50,000.03 for Crypto Currency Partners LP, totaling $3,250,001.93 across three fund entities. These are distinct records and must not be conflated with the separate $3 million IGO investment without reconciliation.

The DOJ document family includes EFTA00900908 and EFTA00901772. The Washington Post's February 3, 2026 report states that Epstein's Coinbase investment was $3 million through IGO LLC, represented less than one percent of Coinbase, and gave him no governance role. It reports that he retained the stake through 2017 and sold half in February 2018 for $15 million. The underlying 2018 offer and closing/payment records must be acquired and reconciled before treating the reported exit value as a verified cash receipt.

A separate 2014 asset valuation record indexed as EFTA00617222 lists “Coinbase” at $3,001,000. This supports the existence of a recorded asset valuation, not by itself the amount ultimately realized on exit.

### 2.3 Blockstream and other crypto-company investments

A Blockchain Capital Crypto Currency Partners II presentation dated October 2015 lists a $100,000 October 2014 Series A preferred investment in Blockstream and a $200,000 December 2014 Coinbase Series C preferred investment among the fund's “12 new investments.” It separately lists eight contributed investments totaling $470,000. The schedule is a fund portfolio document; it does not, standing alone, prove Epstein personally supplied each portfolio investment or establish his precise look-through ownership.

Other DOJ-indexed asset schedules and secondary reporting refer to a Blockstream investment routed through Kyara Investments III LLC, with reported figures around $500,000. This is a separate attribution trail from the Blockchain Capital fund schedule. The amounts and vehicles must remain separate until source agreements, bank transfers, capital accounts, and ownership records establish whether they represent different investments, overlapping exposures, or a reporting/classification discrepancy.

The investigation will also inventory other cryptocurrency investments named in the fund and asset schedules, including PeerNova, ChangeTip, BitPesa, itBit, Ripple, ABRA, Chain, Gem, BitGo, Kraken, LedgerX, BitAccess, BitNet, GoCoin, and Coinsetter. Inclusion in a fund schedule is not automatically proof of Epstein's direct personal investment.

### 2.4 Satoshi Nakamoto / authorship claim

A viral image purporting to show an October 31, 2008 Epstein email to Ghislaine Maxwell stating that the “Satoshi” pseudonym was working has been assessed by AFP as fabricated. AFP identifies formatting anomalies and the use of the `terramar.org` email domain, which postdates the alleged 2008 message. Lead Stories separately reports that the purported message and its quoted phrases do not appear in the released records.

No credible evidence identified in this initial review establishes Epstein as Satoshi Nakamoto or as a creator of Bitcoin. This remains an open evidence-testing question, not a positive attribution. The investigation must inspect authentic Satoshi-related messages in the released corpus individually and distinguish incidental mention, forwarded content, quoted material, and direct correspondence.

## 8. Independent investigation into Bitcoin's creator

The identity question is a legitimate research objective, but it must be investigated independently of Epstein's financial connections. The Bitcoin-specific workstream will therefore have two parallel tracks:

- **Epstein intersection track:** determine whether Epstein-related records establish any connection to early Bitcoin development, developers, funding, or Satoshi communications.
- **Independent origin track:** reconstruct the technical and documentary history of Bitcoin's creation, regardless of whether Epstein appears in that history.

The public record currently establishes that Satoshi Nakamoto is a pseudonym and that the person or people behind it created the white paper and initial software. The actual civil identity or identities have not been established publicly. The UK High Court's 2024 COPA v Wright judgment rejected Craig Wright's claim and declared that he was not Satoshi, did not create Bitcoin, and did not author the initial software. That judgment excludes Wright as the creator; it does not identify the actual creator. See the official judgment: https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/COPA-v-Wright-Judgment.pdf.

The origin investigation will reconstruct:
1. The intellectual and technical precursors: public-key cryptography, digital cash, Chaumian e-cash, Hashcash, b-money, Bit Gold, timestamp chains, reusable proof-of-work, and related work.
2. The first authenticated Satoshi communications, including the August 2008 correspondence with Adam Back and Wei Dai, the October 31, 2008 white-paper announcement, and the early 2009 software release and network launch.
3. The initial codebase, design decisions, commit history, build artifacts, protocol changes, and handoff of project maintenance.
4. The known communication network among Satoshi, early developers, cryptographers, mailing-list participants, and later maintainers.
5. Candidate claims and counterclaims, tested against authenticated records rather than online speculation.

The 2024 judgment's historical summary describes Satoshi contacting Adam Back in August 2008 about a draft and a Hashcash citation, then contacting Wei Dai about b-money; it records the white-paper announcement on October 31, 2008. See paragraphs 22–23 of the judgment. These are important chronological anchors, not proof of Satoshi's civil identity.

The investigation will not name a living person as Satoshi based on linguistic resemblance, time zone, technical ability, ideological alignment, association, or circumstantial timing. Strong attribution would require decisive evidence such as a verifiable signature from a key reliably tied to Satoshi, authenticated contemporaneous records, or equivalent direct evidence independently corroborated.

This independent line may generate useful leads about the technical origins of Bitcoin even if it never identifies the person behind the pseudonym. It must remain analytically separate from the Epstein inquiry so that Epstein's later investments or contacts do not bias the search.
